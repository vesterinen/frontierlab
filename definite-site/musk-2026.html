The take

Three claims in forty seconds, and they need three completely different tests. That’s exactly why we file things instead of just sharing them.

The intelligence claim sounds definite and isn’t: “the sum of all human intelligence” has no agreed measure, so as stated it can’t be scored. It stays on file as unfalsifiable until its author names the test.

The robots claim is gloriously falsifiable — and mathematically brutal. Cumulative humanoid production today is measured in thousands. A hundred million by 2031 requires a faster manufacturing ramp than anything in industrial history, smartphones included. This is the one to watch.

The doubling claim is directional, and the most boring instrument in the world will answer it: GDP statistics.

One more thing. A fabricated version of these remarks — “three years left to make money selling your work” — spread faster than the real footage. He never said it. That gap between what spreads and what was said is why this record exists.

Claim 1 — intelligence

Digital intelligence exceeds the sum of all human intelligence by ~2031. Status: unfalsifiable as stated. No agreed measure of “the sum” exists; the entry is held open pending a testable formulation.

Claim 2 — robots

100 million to 1 billion humanoid robots within five years.

  • Signposts, 18 months: actual production rates at Optimus, Figure, 1X; announced factory capacity
  • Kill condition: cumulative units under ~1 million by end-2027 — the 100M-by-2031 path is arithmetically dead
Claim 3 — economy

An economic doubling period begins — output grows fast enough that “giant changes” are visible within years.

  • Signpost: global GDP growth rates visibly departing from the post-war 2–4% band
  • Kill condition: growth stays in the historical band through the claimed window
